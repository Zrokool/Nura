The problem

The missing layer

Sudan, the Sahel and East Africa lack light infrastructure: the small, local public works that everyday life depends on. That means mini-grids, clean-water boreholes, neighbourhood clinics, community telecoms and streetlights.

The Organisation of Islamic Cooperation (OIC) has warned that chronic shortages of this decentralised infrastructure make the region's climate shocks, displacement and insecurity worse. Heavy infrastructure takes decades. Light infrastructure can be rebuilt in weeks, if communities are allowed to own it.

Why the lights go out

Three forces keep the dark

Systematic destruction

In active conflict, including the war in Sudan, armed factions such as the RSF target local energy and communications assets. Water networks fail with them, because the pumps need power.

Impact · Up to 40% dark

Rural–urban disparity

Even before the war, city centres had patchy grid access while rural communities had almost none. Central grids never reach the villages, camps and edges of towns where people now live.

Impact · ~50% no water point

Climate–scarcity loop

SESRIC describes a vicious cycle. Without decentralised water and energy management, land degrades and droughts deepen. Economies weaken, and there is even less to build with.

Impact · Self-reinforcing
Who pays for the dark

Everyone deserves the safety of light

Streetlights pave the way for travel. Without them, the most ordinary trips become the most dangerous.

  • Children walking to school, to the suuq and between family homes, often before sunrise and after sunset.
  • Women who carry the caregiving of whole families: preparing food, supporting children's education and reaching medical care, often at night.
  • Patients and health workers moving between homes and clinics when emergencies don't wait for daylight.
  • Displaced families in camps and host towns where paths, latrines and water points sit in total darkness.
The impact of Nura

Light without the overhead

Nura removes the expensive parts of a streetlight: trenching, grid connections, central control rooms and corporate contracts. It keeps what matters: light that stays on, and a community that knows the moment it doesn't.
