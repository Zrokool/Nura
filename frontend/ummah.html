Ummah · Why it matters

One body,
one light

“The believers, in their mutual kindness, compassion and sympathy, are like one body.” The darkness in El Fasher, Niamey and Mogadishu is one wound. Nura is built for the Sahel and East Africa together.

The belt

From Dakar to Mogadishu

The Sahel runs 6,000 km from the Atlantic to the Red Sea. Sudan joins it to East Africa, and Somalia carries it to the Horn. Across all of it: the same heat, the same droughts, the same fractured grids and the same families living at the edge of the light.

Country by country

Same night, different causes

East Africa Pilot focus

SD · Sudan

War on the grid

Power stations and communications assets have been hit repeatedly, including near Port Sudan and Kassala. Water pumps and clinic power go down with them.

SO · Somalia

Drought & displacement

Droughts and decades of conflict have pushed families into settlements on city edges, beyond the grid, where paths and water points are unlit.

The Sahel

TD · Chad

Hosting the displaced

Eastern Chad hosts large numbers of Sudanese refugees in camps and border towns, and spans the Lake Chad Basin.

ML · BF · NE

Central Sahel

In Mali, Burkina Faso and Niger, insecurity has closed schools and clinics. Decentralised power is often the only kind that survives.

NG · Nigeria

The northeast

Displacement around the Lake Chad Basin stretches host communities. Night trips to water, latrines and clinics carry real risk.

SN · Senegal

The western anchor

The Atlantic end of the Dakar–Port Sudan corridor, and a stable place for fabrication, training and early manufacturing.

What the OIC is doing

From statements to corridors

The OIC has moved from purely political positions to funding physical "light" and "heavy" corridors to stabilise the region.

Healthcare

Islamic Solidarity Fund

Delivering modern diagnostic and medical equipment directly to 80 local health centres across 11 Sudanese states to strengthen grassroots care.

Heavy links

Dakar–Port Sudan Railway

A planned 10,100 km line across Sudan, Chad, Nigeria, Niger, Burkina Faso, Mali and Senegal. It would turn isolated rural corridors into economic zones where light local industry can trade.

10,100 km · 7 nations

Focus · Emergency
What still blocks progress

Two barriers. One design answer.

New local infrastructure is still sabotaged within days, and thousands of small projects can't get financed. Nura was designed against both.

BarrierTodayNura's answer
Security geopolitics No peacekeeping presence, so new assets are sabotaged. Large central assets are single points of failure. Hundreds of lights at about $250 installed, with no substation to strike. Each light is independent. The AI flags a destroyed pole within minutes, and a local fixer restores it within a day.
Funding bottleneck Thousands of decentralised hubs need intense micro-financing. The OIC looks to outside partners such as China's Belt and Road Initiative. A price point that suits micro-finance: one pole per donor, one street per masjid, one zone per city budget. Owned by the city, not a corporation.
