Inaccuracies in the solution
The ping rule has a typo: "A can't ping A" should read "A can't ping B".
The rule fails in three cases:
Lights at the end of a street have only one neighbour, so the rule can never fire.
A light whose two links are both blocked looks dead even though it works.
A neighbour that is itself dead is treated as evidence.
The chosen 20 W MPPT kit has no load output. The lamp has to be wired through the battery and a 12→5 V converter.
Resolved: the magnetic kitchen light is replaced by a 10 W, 12 V, IP65 LED lamp (~1,200 lumens) on an M12 IP68 waterproof plug. The power system is resized to a 30 W panel and a 12 V 12 Ah battery: about 81 Wh needed a day against about 120 Wh harvested, with 1.5 nights of reserve (about 2.4 in low-battery mode). Updated across the site, dashboard, Nebo.md, AI docs, sample data and 3D model.
Ollama should not decide outages. Language models can make things up, so the rules decide and the AI only explains.
One 3D-printed piece can't hold the panel. The panel (381 × 335 mm) is bigger than most printer beds, and PLA softens at about 55 °C. The case is now modular and printed in ASA.
Resolved: the site now shows both costs, about $170 in parts and about $250 installed ($232–277 for a 60-light pilot: parts, shipping and duty, labour, gateway share and spares). New poles are listed separately at $60–150.

Gaps in the solution
Theft: panels and batteries are worth money, so theft is more likely than sabotage. Plan for security screws, engraved parts and community ownership.
Poles: the plan assumes metal poles already exist. Many camps and villages have none, so budget for poles and foundations.
Blackout mode: in active conflict, lit streets can help attackers or drones at night. The committee needs a way to switch a zone off instantly.
Supply chain: Amazon doesn't ship to Sudan. Sanctions and import rules make local or regional sourcing necessary.
Fixers: there is no plan yet for their pay, training, tools or stock of spares. This will drive repair times more than any technology.
Upkeep: dust on panels cuts power, so panels need regular cleaning. Batteries wear out in about 5–8 years and need replacing and recycling.
Governance: it isn't settled who owns the system when the city government is contested.
Gateway: each zone depends on one gateway, which needs its own solar power, a safe spot and a backup.
Radio licence: the radio needs approval from the national regulator for its frequency band.
Lighting standard: there are no targets yet for brightness, pole height or spacing.
Impact measures: nothing yet tracks lit hours, school attendance, clinic visits or safety reports.
